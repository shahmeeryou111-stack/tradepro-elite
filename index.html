Trading Terminology

Trading Glossary

Essential trading terms and definitions every trader should know.

Pip

The smallest price movement in Forex, typically the fourth decimal place (0.0001). For JPY pairs, it's the second decimal place (0.01).

Lot

Standard unit of trading. A standard lot = 100,000 units, mini lot = 10,000 units, micro lot = 1,000 units of the base currency.

Spread

The difference between the bid (buy) and ask (sell) price. Lower spreads mean lower trading costs.

Leverage

Using borrowed capital to increase potential returns. Expressed as a ratio (e.g., 1:100 means $100 controls $10,000).

Margin

The required collateral to open and maintain leveraged positions. It's not a fee but a portion of your account balance set aside.

Stop Loss

An order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price to limit potential losses.

Take Profit

An order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price to secure profits.

Bid Price

The price at which the market/broker is willing to buy a currency pair from you (selling price).

Ask Price

The price at which the market/broker is willing to sell a currency pair to you (buying price).

Long Position

Buying a currency pair with the expectation that the price will rise. Profit if price goes up, loss if price goes down.

Short Position

Selling a currency pair with the expectation that the price will fall. Profit if price goes down, loss if price goes up.

Support Level

A price level where buying interest is strong enough to prevent further price declines. Acts as a floor.

Resistance Level

A price level where selling pressure is strong enough to prevent further price increases. Acts as a ceiling.

Bull Market

A market condition characterized by rising prices and optimism. Traders are generally buying.

Bear Market

A market condition characterized by falling prices and pessimism. Traders are generally selling.

Volatility

The degree of price variation over time. High volatility means large price swings, low volatility means stable prices.

Liquidity

The ease with which an asset can be bought or sold without affecting its price. High liquidity means tight spreads and easy execution.

Fundamental Analysis

Analyzing economic indicators, central bank policies, and geopolitical events to predict price movements.

Technical Analysis

Analyzing price charts, patterns, and indicators to identify trading opportunities based on historical price action.

Market Order

An order to buy or sell immediately at the current market price. Execution is guaranteed but price may vary slightly.

Limit Order

An order to buy or sell at a specified price or better. Only executes if the market reaches that price.

Slippage

The difference between the expected price of a trade and the price at which it's actually executed, common during high volatility.

Drawdown

The peak-to-trough decline during a specific period. Measures the decline from a historical peak in account value.

Risk/Reward Ratio

The relationship between the potential loss (risk) and potential profit (reward) of a trade. A 1:2 ratio means risking $1 to make $2.

Position Sizing

Determining the number of lots/units to trade based on account size, risk percentage, and stop loss distance.

Scalping

A trading strategy that aims to profit from small price changes, typically holding positions for seconds to minutes.

Day Trading

Opening and closing positions within the same trading day, avoiding overnight exposure to market risk.

Swing Trading

Holding positions for several days to weeks to capture larger price movements and market trends.
